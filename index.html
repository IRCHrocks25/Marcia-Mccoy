The Visualizer's Dilemma

Your Work Is Brilliant. Why Is It Still Sitting in Your Studio?

You know the feeling. A blank screen at midnight. A tutorial full of words that were never meant for a creative mind. The quiet dread that the world is moving on without you.

I have sat across from hundreds of visionaries with exactly this fear. It is never a lack of talent. It is never a lack of ideas. It is a lack of confidence.

When I stepped back from agenting full time, the calls didn't stop. People I'd worked with for decades still wanted me representing them. That's where this began: I realized I could teach them to do for themselves what I used to do for them.

Here is the truth I learned agenting: great work sitting in a studio that no one sees serves no one. By my own count, more than half the collections I watched over four decades ended up in storage, or worse, the dump, because no one built a system to share them while there was still time.

People do not need more technology. They need more confidence. That is what AI for Rookies gives you first.

I did not learn that lesson in a workshop. I learned it seventeen years ago, in a hospital bed, and I will tell you that story before we are through. For now, know this: confidence is not something you are born with. I have watched people build it in real time. I built mine the same way.

The Gordon Parks Legacy

The lesson behind everything I teach

What Twenty-Three Years and Seven Hundred Thousand Dollars Taught Me

For thirty-three years, Gordon Parks was my friend. I marketed his first traveling exhibition, two hundred photographs that toured and sold out to museums for twenty-three years straight. He never finished high school. He became the first African American photographer to document the Civil Rights Movement for Life magazine, and the first Black director Hollywood ever commissioned, for The Learning Tree and then Shaft.

But Gordon had a second life the world refused to see. In his final thirty years, once he finally had the means to stop shooting on the street, he made fine art. Paintings. Drawings. A body of work he considered his truest voice.

When his retrospective catalog, Half Past Autumn, came out, it ran three hundred sixty pages. His fine art got twelve of them.

I spent twenty-three years and seven hundred thousand dollars of my own money trying to change that. Contracts. Gallery meetings. Museum pitches, one slow negotiation at a time. Gordon passed at ninety-three, in the middle of our launch, still believing the world had never fully accepted him as an artist.

I could not move fast enough back then. Every step was manual, and manual takes decades.

What Actually Holds When Everything Else Falls Apart

"I'm a creator, not a tech person. Can I really do this?"

I wasn't a tech person either. I hired five teachers and gave myself fourteen-hour days over one Christmas break to learn this the hard way, so you don't have to. Confidence over coding. No programming required, ever.

"What about protecting my rights and my work?"

When Gordon Parks died in the middle of our launch, the one thing that held was a simple, one-page contract I'd written years earlier, the kind that survives an estate. I call it my Cover Your Ass contract. It's yours too, built into the Playbook.

"Should I wait until AI settles down?"

I didn't wait for it to settle. I gave myself four weeks and started anyway, and it still hasn't settled. The best time to start was yesterday. The next best time is today.

The Miracle Scans

Waking up to a miracle

I told you I would come back to this.

Seventeen years ago, I was semiconscious in intensive care for ten days. In that place between here and gone, I made a promise. I asked to come back and finish what I started, but only if I was granted complete healing.

I woke up. The scans were perfect. My doctors had no explanation. I did not need one.

That was my first defining moment. My second came more recently, and quietly, one loss at a time. Thirteen people in my inner circle died within a few years of each other, including both of my parents and the mentors who shaped my entire career.

I stopped and asked myself a hard question: what do I still want to build, and who am I still meant to help, before my own time runs out?

The answer became my Ten Legacy Projects. AI for Rookies is the first one I am offering in service, because it is the fastest way to help the most people help themselves.
